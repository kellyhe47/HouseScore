Data & Ethics — how the House Score works

This page is the score's one-page rationale: what we predict, which signals we use, the exact weights, what we deliberately do not do, and how the pipeline was evaluated.

Who the score finds (ICP)

The best concierge customer: recently moved in, hires out rather than DIYs, has capacity to pay for years of service, and has near-term service need. Each signal below traces to one of those four traits — nothing in the score exists without an ICP reason.

{{ row.g }} {{ row.max }} {{ row.d }}

score = clamp(mover + hires-out + capacity + need + modifier, 0, 100). Deterministic, fixture-tested (12 golden fixtures, run by make eval). Missing fields degrade gracefully to confidence: low with an explicit data-gap evidence line — never a crash, never a guess.

What we deliberately do not do

  • Daniel's Law: owner names and mailing addresses arrive redacted from NJ MOD-IV and stay that way. We never attempt identity reconstruction — no deed-book name mining, no broker data. Nothing in this UI ever shows who lives at a door.
  • No household inference from Census data: ACS evidence is always phrased as neighborhood context ("block group: 41% dual-income"), never as a claim about a household.
  • Street View ToS: Google Maps Platform ToS §3.2.3 prohibits derived datasets, so bulk imagery signals come from public-domain NJ orthophotos and NAIP. Street View is used at demo scale (~50 doors) with this position published. Zillow/Redfin excluded (ToS), cited.
  • Absentee detection uses only a municipal rental-registration match — a mild −15, because rental households still buy services. Never identity-derived.
